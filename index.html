Solutions We Explored

Pendant-Style Wearable

  • Positioned near the throat for stable and consistent sensing
  • Captures voice input, throat vibrations, and environmental data together
  • Allows integration of multiple sensors in a compact wearable form
  • Functions as both technology and an everyday accessory
  • Comfortable and lightweight for long performances and daily use
  • Slightly farther from the throat compared to direct-contact solutions
  • External noise interference may reduce voice-reading accuracy

Choker-Style Wearable

  • Explored for close throat contact and accurate vibration sensing
  • Designed to blend naturally with stage and performance aesthetics
  • Provides stable sensor placement during movement
  • Provides real-time feedback through vibration and LED indicators
  • Enables continuous monitoring and real-time feedback
  • Compact and minimal wearable structure
  • Suitable for singers and performance-focused users
  • Practical for continuous monitoring without interrupting performances
  • Possibly less comfortable for prolonged use due to tight neck placement

Adhesive Patch System

  • Lightweight, minimal and discreet wearable design
  • Direct skin contact improves vibration detection accuracy
  • Suitable for focused short-term monitoring sessions
  • Allows close placement near vocal vibration points
  • Limited by placement consistency and repeated application
  • Potential concerns regarding skin irritation and long-term comfort
  • Limited space for integrating larger sensors or batteries
  • Less practical for continuous everyday monitoring

Final Solution and Why We Chose It

Why the Choker Was Selected

  • The choker-style wearable provided the closest and most stable positioning near the throat.
  • Its placement allowed more accurate sensing of throat vibrations and vocal activity.
  • The design enabled continuous monitoring without requiring the user to hold or adjust the device.
  • It blended naturally with stage and performance aesthetics, making it more acceptable for singers and performers.
  • The compact wearable form allowed integration of multiple sensors while remaining lightweight.
  • Unlike adhesive patches, it avoided repeated application and positioning inconsistencies.
  • Compared to pendant-style designs, it maintained more reliable proximity to the vocal source during movement.

Final Design Direction

  • A wearable choker-based vocal health monitoring system.
  • Designed to detect early signs of vocal strain in real time.
  • Combines vocal sensing, throat vibration monitoring, and environmental analysis.
  • Provides immediate feedback through non-intrusive alerts.
  • Encourages preventive vocal care and healthier voice usage habits.

In Summary

Based on the identified opportunities, evaluated concepts, and user-focused requirements, the team finalized the choker-style wearable as the most effective solution for stable sensing, continuous monitoring, and real-world usability. The next phase focused on developing a functional prototype integrating sensing, processing, and feedback systems.

Our Product — VocaWatch

A Smart Wearable System for Real-Time Vocal Health Monitoring

Intelligent Vocal Care Through Smart Wearable Technology

VocaWatch is a wearable vocal-health monitoring system designed to help singers, speakers, teachers and performers protect their voice through intelligent real-time monitoring. The wearable choker form factor allows stable throat positioning, enabling accurate vibration sensing and continuous feedback. By combining throat sensing, voice intensity monitoring, environmental sensing and intelligent processing, the system provides preventive care before severe strain occurs.

Hardware Components

ESP32 Development Board

The ESP32 acts as the brain of the system. It collects sensor data, processes logic, controls alerts and manages system operation.

MiniSense 100 Sensor

Detects throat vibrations during speaking and provides an EGG-inspired sensing approach for vocal activity monitoring.

MAX9814 Microphone

Measures voice loudness and speaking intensity to help identify overuse, shouting and excessive vocal pressure.

DHT22 Sensor

Measures temperature and humidity conditions to support hydration reminders and dryness detection.

MQ135 Air Quality Sensor

Detects smoke, pollutants and poor air quality which may contribute to throat irritation and vocal discomfort.

Coin Vibration Motor

Generates silent haptic feedback alerts for reminders and warnings without interrupting users.

3.7V LiPo Battery

Powers the wearable system and enables portable usage during performances and long speaking sessions.

TP4056 Charging Module

Provides safe USB charging functionality for the wearable battery system.

AMS1117 Voltage Regulator

Ensures stable 3.3V power delivery to the ESP32 and connected sensors.

System Assembly

1 Velcro strap and foam layer create a stable and comfortable wearable base.
2 MiniSense sensor is centrally positioned for throat vibration sensing.
3 ESP32 is mounted as the central processing unit.
4 Sensors are positioned outward for airflow and sound exposure.
5 Vibration motor provides discreet feedback alerts.
6 Wiring is routed along the strap for compact wearable integration.

The Impact

Expected impact of VocaWatch

The expected impact of this solution is to shift vocal health management from a reactive approach to a proactive one. Instead of recognizing vocal damage only after discomfort becomes severe, users can identify early warning signs and respond immediately.

This reduces the risk of long-term vocal strain, throat irritation, and potential voice disorders. The system also promotes consistent healthy habits such as proper voice usage, regular breaks, and hydration.

By providing objective, real-time feedback, it removes dependence on subjective self-assessment and increases awareness of how both voice usage and environmental conditions affect vocal health.

Overall, it aims to improve well-being, enhance performance quality, and support individuals who rely heavily on their voice in daily life.

User Journey with the Product

A typical user begins by wearing the device during daily activities such as speaking, teaching, or singing. As they use their voice, the device continuously monitors vocal activity, throat vibrations, and surrounding environmental conditions like air quality and humidity.

When the system detects excessive voice usage, strain, or harmful conditions, it provides immediate feedback through simple alerts such as vibrations or light signals.

This prompts the user to take corrective actions, such as reducing voice intensity, taking breaks, or hydrating.

Over time, the user becomes more aware of their vocal habits and starts making better decisions naturally, leading to healthier and more sustainable voice usage without relying solely on guesswork.

Real-Life Examples & Relevance

Vocal strain and voice-related injuries are not uncommon, even among highly trained professionals. For instance, Adele had to undergo surgery for a vocal cord hemorrhage after prolonged strain and intense performances. Similarly, Sam Smith experienced a vocal cord hemorrhage that forced them to cancel multiple tours and take complete vocal rest. John Mayer faced recurring issues with vocal cord granulomas, which significantly impacted his ability to perform and required long recovery periods.

These cases highlight a common pattern—continuous voice usage, high intensity, and delayed response to early warning signs. In many situations, symptoms such as hoarseness, throat dryness, or increased effort while singing appear early but are often overlooked until the condition becomes severe.

The ‘show must go on’ mentality often acts as a double-edged sword, where performers prioritize immediate obligations over long-term physiological health, effectively masking pain until it becomes a pathology.

A system that provides real-time monitoring and feedback could play a crucial role in such scenarios by making users more aware of these early signs. For example, if a singer during rehearsals is alerted about increasing vocal strain or unhealthy usage patterns, they can adjust their technique, take breaks, or prevent further damage.

While such a device cannot replace medical diagnosis or eliminate risk entirely, it can act as an early warning and awareness tool, helping reduce the likelihood of long-term vocal injury.

Beyond the stage, teachers face a parallel risk as they must constantly project over ambient classroom noise for hours on end. For these educators, a real-time monitoring tool could act as a vital safeguard, providing the feedback needed to prevent daily vocal fatigue from evolving into career-ending chronic conditions.

These examples demonstrate that vocal health is a significant concern even at the highest levels, reinforcing the need for accessible tools that support preventive care and informed voice usage.
